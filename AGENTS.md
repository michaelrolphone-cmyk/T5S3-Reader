## Hardware/driver ownership: sole I²C exception

Read [Hardware Boundary](docs/HARDWARE_AGNOSTIC_DRIVER_BOUNDARY.md) and [I²C Bus ELF Cutover](docs/I2C_BOOTSTRAP_CUTOVER.md) before hardware, driver, transport, device or registry changes. The generic core knows opaque capability identifiers and provides loading, context/permission, registry/resolver, streams and lifecycle; it does not implement/select USB, GNSS or other devices. USB host/class/chipset/session/transfer and actual device power/charger/expander register policy live in ELFs. Thin peripheral proxies into firmware fail architecture.

**One deliberate transitional root-bus exception:** from U1 the independently installed **I²C bus ELF** publishes the standard versioned `i2c.bus` capability. It is the ONLY production ELF allowed to import/call the private, bounded and versioned temporary firmware I²C controller/transfer API. Power, expander, USB-supporting, RTC, touch and all other I²C device ELFs require the **bus ELF capability** and cannot directly import firmware I²C/`Wire`/`kernel.i2c`; their device logic is entirely native. The bus ELF mediates client rights/serialization and alone delegates its temporary raw controller implementation to the port. Missing bus ELF means missing bus, never firmware fallback. Record the private import allowlist and noncyclic startup. **U3 replaces the same I²C ELF's internal controller backend and removes firmware API/imports while keeping public bus ABI and already-migrated peripheral ELFs stable.** Do not require a full native-controller I²C ELF for U1; do require the real installed bus ELF/provider now. Isolated ROM/boot/recovery paths cannot register as production fallback.

CPU/ABI port differences may require a new port, while compatible new devices require only ELF/profile; use execution-context-owned generation-safe handles. Installing a manifest does not grant hardware access. Use RiscRTE for platform, CrossPoint for reader subsystem only.
